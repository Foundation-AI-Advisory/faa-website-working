Delivered. Accepted. Still Not Invoiced.

By Benjamin DeMichael · October 24, 2026

Completed work can wait on missing evidence and unclear handoffs. Make billing readiness visible before automating invoice preparation.

In this article
Define when the work is actually billableFind where evidence stops movingBuild a daily view of billing readinessApply AI to preparation and follow-up

The work is done. The customer has the product or the completed service. The invoice has not gone out.

Sometimes that delay is required by the agreement. Sometimes it is a missing document, a disputed quantity, an unapproved change, or a handoff nobody owns.

Before looking for AI to improve collections, I would examine the time between a billable event and a valid invoice reaching the customer. The business cannot collect against an invoice it has not issued, and sending an incomplete one can create another round of delay.

Define when the work is actually billable

Start with the agreement and the operating event that supports billing. That may be shipment, delivery, customer acceptance, completion of a service, or an approved project milestone. The correct trigger depends on the arrangement.

Finance and the responsible operating leader should agree on the definition. Do not treat delivery alone as permission to bill when approval or supporting evidence is still required.

For each eligible item, identify the date the trigger occurred, the required evidence, the invoice status, and the person responsible for the next action. Separate genuinely incomplete work from completed work waiting on administrative steps.

This is an operating review of billing readiness. It does not replace the company’s accounting policies for revenue recognition or its contract review process.

Find where evidence stops moving

A shipping confirmation may exist in the warehouse system while accounting waits for proof of delivery. A field technician may have completed the work but not submitted the service record. A project manager may know a change was approved while the supporting email remains outside the billing file.

Ask the billing team to walk through recent delayed invoices with operations. Find the exact document or decision that was missing. Record who could have supplied it and when the business first knew it was needed.

That review often reveals that people are doing their own tasks correctly while the handoff between them is incomplete. A faster invoice-writing tool will not repair that handoff.

Build a daily view of billing readiness

Data Curation & Governance comes first: consistent customer and job references, agreed billing triggers, approved pricing, and traceable completion or acceptance records. Define which source is authoritative when quantities or status disagree.

Workflow Optimization should establish a named owner for each unresolved item and a routine for clearing it. The controller owns the billing-ready definition and financial controls. Operations owns timely completion evidence. The account or project owner resolves commercial questions.

The queue should show a reason and next action, not simply an aging total. That gives leadership operational visibility into what is holding cash conversion back.

Apply AI to preparation and follow-up

Once the rules and handoffs are clear, AI can assist with organizing supporting records, summarizing why an item is blocked, and drafting a request to the responsible person. Any proposed invoice should reconcile to approved quantities, prices, and terms before release.

Missing evidence should remain visible. The system must not invent an acceptance date or treat an unanswered email as approval. Keep final release and disputed items within the company’s established approval process.

Have the controller lead a four-week review of one delivery or service stream. Start by listing completed items that have not been invoiced and confirming which are actually eligible. Assign the unresolved items, then measure time from the agreed billing trigger to invoice issuance, rejection rates, and the value waiting on each cause.

The intended impact is shorter billing cycle time and better cash-flow visibility. Faster invoicing can support earlier collection, but it does not guarantee customer payment or create additional revenue by itself. Use the measured bottleneck and expected benefit to decide whether an AI implementation is justified and who will maintain it.

The first improvement may be a cleaner handoff between two people. That is still a business result worth delivering.

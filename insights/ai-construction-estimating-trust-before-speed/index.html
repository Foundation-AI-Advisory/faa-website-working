Faster Estimates Only Help If You Can Trust the Number

By Benjamin DeMichael · October 24, 2026

A faster takeoff does not make a trustworthy estimate. Start with labor assumptions, scope, review ownership, and the job the business must actually build.

A contractor can turn an estimate around in an hour and still lose money on the job.

That is what concerns me about the way AI estimating is being sold. The conversation often starts with how quickly the system can read drawings, count devices, or produce a proposal. Those are useful capabilities. But the business needs an estimate it can build the job against.

The objective is to reduce estimating cycle time while protecting the margin carried into the field. That takes more than a faster takeoff.

Find the assumptions that change the job

Before choosing an AI tool, walk one completed project from the original bid through the final job-cost review. Put the estimator and the field supervisor in the same room.

Where did the labor allowance come from? Did it reflect the crew that actually performed the work? Were access restrictions, installation height, occupied space, travel, supervision, and mobilization included? Which drawing revision was priced? What scope was assumed to be someone else’s responsibility?

A count of 100 fixtures tells you very little about the labor required to install them without that context. A system that produces a confident number from incomplete assumptions can make a bad estimate look finished.

Build a usable estimating foundation

Our sequence is Data Curation & Governance, Workflow Optimization, then AI Design & Implementation.

For estimating, the first step is a controlled set of labor units, material descriptions, supplier quotes, and historical job results. Each needs a source, an effective date, and an owner. Separate ordinary production hours from rework and unusual site conditions before treating past performance as a reliable standard.

Next, define how an estimate moves from intake to scope review, takeoff, pricing, field input, approval, and submission. Record unresolved questions explicitly. An allowance should remain an allowance until someone confirms it.

The chief estimator should own the estimating method. Operations should validate production assumptions. Purchasing should confirm the currency and exclusions of supplier pricing. The person accountable for the bid must be able to explain the number.

Give AI a bounded job

Once that foundation is in place, AI can assist with organizing bid documents, drafting scope summaries, comparing revisions, and preparing a list of apparent omissions for review. Where extraction or quantity tools are used, their output still needs to be checked against the actual documents.

Keep the source page or drawing reference next to each material assumption. Require the system to distinguish supplied facts, calculated values, and missing information. Do not let a missing labor rate quietly become a guessed one.

The estimator retains final approval. A material revision, an unfamiliar assembly, or an unresolved scope conflict should send the work back for review. The production workflow needs a clear way to stop, correct, and resubmit an estimate.

Prove it against work you understand

Have the chief estimator lead a 30-day assessment using a small set of completed jobs and one clearly defined type of new work. Rebuild the estimates using the proposed process and compare the results with the original assumptions and actual performance.

Track preparation time, review time, omissions found, and estimate-to-actual labor variance. Separate differences caused by estimating from changes in scope or field execution. Finance should approve the cost of moving into production against the expected benefit; faster preparation alone is not enough.

The business impact should show up in shorter bid cycle time, less rework, and better control of margin risk. Expand only when the estimate is traceable, the reviewer can defend it, and there is an owner for maintaining the inputs.

I would rather submit a sound estimate a little later than submit an unexplained number immediately. The goal is to win work the business can execute profitably.

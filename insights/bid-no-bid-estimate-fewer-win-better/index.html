Estimate Fewer Jobs. Win Better Work.

By Benjamin DeMichael · October 24, 2026

Estimating capacity should go to work the business can win and execute profitably. Put a clear qualification decision ahead of the detailed estimate.

In this article
Qualification is an operating decisionUse past bids carefullyPut the gate before the detailed estimateUse AI to prepare the decision

A busy estimating department can still be spending its time on the wrong opportunities.

Every invitation looks like potential revenue. Every deadline feels immediate. Eventually, the team is working late to price jobs the company should have declined at intake.

Before using AI to increase estimating volume, I would ask which opportunities deserve the team’s time. Estimating capacity is limited. The business should allocate it deliberately.

Qualification is an operating decision

Bid/no-bid decisions often live in a quick conversation with the owner or salesperson. That experience matters, but the next estimator cannot apply a decision rule that was never made explicit.

Start with the work the company performs well. Consider project type, geography, scope clarity, customer relationship, delivery requirements, crew availability, and the ability to execute at an acceptable margin. Include the effort required to prepare a credible estimate.

A large potential contract can be a poor fit. A smaller repeat project may offer a clearer scope, a better operating fit, and a more realistic path to profitable work. Revenue size alone should not settle the decision.

The commercial leader should own qualification, with operations and estimating responsible for validating feasibility. Material contract or payment concerns should go to the appropriate finance or legal reviewer before the company commits to bid.

Use past bids carefully

Data Curation & Governance starts with a usable bid history. Record what was quoted, the type of customer and work, estimating effort, the decision, and the reason for a win, loss, or withdrawal when known.

Distinguish a confirmed loss from a job with no reported outcome. Separate work lost on price from work the company could not staff or deliver. Avoid treating a small or incomplete history as a dependable prediction of win probability.

Actual project results matter too. Winning similar work at an attractive quoted margin means little if the jobs consistently require unpriced supervision or suffer scope disputes.

Finance should help the team compare like work and identify where the records are too weak to support a conclusion.

Put the gate before the detailed estimate

Workflow Optimization means creating an early, lightweight decision. Intake should produce one of three outcomes: proceed, request clarification, or decline.

Give the reviewer enough information to make that decision without commissioning the full takeoff. Set a short response time and a backup approver so the gate does not become another queue. Keep a reason for declining and an escalation path for a strategic exception.

This is not an excuse to make qualification bureaucratic. It is a way to prevent expensive effort from starting by default.

Use AI to prepare the decision

With reliable records and an agreed gate, AI can summarize bid invitations, extract deadlines and apparent scope requirements, and flag missing information against the company’s qualification criteria. A reviewer should verify the extracted details against the source documents.

Keep final acceptance and rejection with the accountable commercial owner. Do not allow a generated score to quietly replace judgment, especially when historical records are sparse or the customer relationship carries context the system cannot see.

Over the next four weeks, have the commercial leader apply the gate to incoming opportunities with the chief estimator and operations manager. Measure estimating hours committed, turnaround time, reasons for declining, and exceptions approved. Track win rate and job margin over the longer period needed for projects to mature.

The intended impact is more estimating throughput on suitable work, shorter cycle time, and better margin discipline. Use the measured reduction in wasted effort to decide whether automation has an economic case and a sustainable production owner.

The goal is not to bid on everything faster. It is to spend the business’s limited capacity where it has a credible chance to earn a return.
